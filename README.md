Many proposals for "universal" all-encompassing mathematical and computational theories of architectures constructed by composing neural networks have been made in recent times as large tech companies find the best way to scale complicated graphs of many component AI models; for example, CalTech research by Marcolli, and the late Yuri Manin proposed in [Homotopy Theoretic and Categorical Models of Neural Information Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15136). While the theory itself is a beautiful methamtical composition, it focuses on a very narrow set of compositions of neural networks, restricting to architectures that are not very helpful for solving modern, advanced, scientific reasoning problems such as those needed for designing new medicine. Here, we present a theory that is both pragmatic and advanced enough in terms of mathematics and computational theory to provide a very comprehensive picture of compositions of neural networks that are more general. In fact, restricting all the way down to the achitecture from [iska-net](https://github.com/amelie-iska/iska-net) (with or without tropical attention), and no further, yields a theory of composed learned functions that are each capable of universal graph-to-graph function approximation when running backprop and gradient descent on the composed architecture, which, is more expressive, general, and powerful than the standard transformer architecture, by adapting standard transformers to graphs with a very simple tokenization method for both vertices and edges. Therefore, in this work, we restrict to the transformer architecture along with various common adapter layers, and we compose AI models by linking their embedding spaces, rather than entering and exiting token space with encoding and decoding layers between the composed AI models. 

This provides practical implementation, training, and inference insights that lead to an "optimal" composition of models given a particular complex scientific reasoning problem, where some models may be more general, while others may be more specialized for a specific subtask. The theory yields a complete geometric description of the linked embedding spaces, described how to backprop through the complete composed architecture, and provides pragmatic optimization techniques for constructing optimal compositions of learned functions under resrouce constraints such as compute time, memory, token throughput, etc. We do not restrict the modaility of the transofrmers, and in fact, due to the universal graph-to-graph function approximation capabilities of such architectures (making them very expressive and generalizable), almost any modality is fair game, including multilingual text, reasoning graphs via "Graph-of-Thought" reasoning (in embedding space), biomolecular data (small molecules, peptides, proteins, RNA, DNA, and any complexes built from them, along with their dynamics and interactions and chemical processes such as catalysis), linguistic parse trees, complicated Hebrew graphs based on roots and their augmentations, images, 3D world models, 2D video, graphics meshes, and any other graph structured data. 

This in turn provides something of a dream scenario for "systems level thinkers", i.e. people managing larger teams, building many different models for various purposes and tasks, looking for a way to optimize the architecture obtained from composing many models under  specific resource constraints with a single, standardized architecture that can be shown to be as expressive and generalizable as one could be for almost any modality, due to the fact that they are universal graph-to-graph function approximators. Since compositions of such universal G2G function approximators is again a universal G2G function approximator, and since everything remains differentiable (and therefore trainable with backprop throughout the total composed system of models). This unified architecture for any set of modalities standardizes the theory in a way that would not be possible if we were to throw in every type of neural network, and yet, remains as expressive and generalizable as the case where we did include all such architectures; in other words, we have a standardizes, unified mathematical theory and proof that complicated compositions remain as expressive and generalizable as possible, and therefore the standardization of the model arch does not sacrifice anything like performace, accuracy, expressiveness, generalizability, resource specs per layer, and other metrics devised to test the quality of the model. 

Moreover, for reasoning agents trained via some form of reinforcement learning, we describe a rigorous generalization of the "assistant-axis" discovered by Anthropic in recent month, and show how to utilize it for more fine-grained, precision control over and steering of model personality and behavior, via tropical geometry in embedding space(s). This provides an unprecedented level of control over model output style and qualities, and due to the generalization of everything being done in embedding space(s), the thoery makes it applicapble to almost any modalities of input and output. 

This paper develops a ground-up research program for *composable learned operators* in which every internal module is viewed through a *tropical lens* and every architecture is represented as a *decorated quiver of embedding spaces*.
The organizing principle is boundary-aware:
discrete token spaces appear only at *source encoder* vertices and *sink decoder* vertices, while all intermediate computation lives in differentiable embedding spaces connected by general adapters, projections, cross-attention interfaces, residual merges, and neural-operator couplings.
This perspective is designed to cover standard transformers and their descendants---including ViT, DiT, SiT, multimodal fusion stacks, diffusion/flow transformers, state-space hybrids, and scientific foundation-model pipelines---while also extending to non-transformer modules.

The mathematical thesis is that modular deep systems should be studied as *quivers of tropicalized operators*.
Piecewise-linear modules induce exact tropical rational maps and activation fans; transformer blocks become compositions of tropicalizable linear projections, piecewise-linear feed-forward layers, and tropical or locally tropicalized attention kernels; smooth modules admit local tropical charts on compact energy shells.
Composing such modules yields *composed tropical spaces*: polyhedral atlases, tropical varieties, and scheme-like glued cell complexes attached to paths and cycles of the quiver.
This makes the geometry of composability explicit rather than hidden inside a global linear surrogate.

For cyclic quivers we develop a joint *tropical-dynamical-ergodic* viewpoint.
Activation itineraries define symbolic dynamics on cell complexes; max-plus and subadditive cocycles quantify long-run growth; invariant measures describe occupancy of activation fans; and Navier--Stokes-inspired "thought-fluid" regularization constrains continuous Graph-of-Thought trajectories in embedding space.
The reward structure is integrated with *hybrid continuous-discrete GFlowNets*: graph construction trajectories and continuous reasoning trajectories are both sampled with probability proportional to multiobjective rewards that favor high-quality solutions subject to explicit resource constraints.
We also show how recent activation-steering ideas, exemplified by the ``Assistant Axis,'' can be lifted to quiver-local tropical steering atlases that regulate both instantaneous embedding updates and long-run ergodic occupancy of activation regions.

The manuscript emphasizes formal definitions, rigorous propositions, implementation-oriented algorithms, and realistic examples.
These include continuous GoT reasoning GFlowNets, multimodal source/sink compositions linking NatureLM-style cross-domain sequence reasoning to MDGen-style SiT generative dynamics, and multi-model therapeutic design cycles that couple generators, evaluators, structure predictors, cell-state models, and phenotype or variant predictors through adapter-mediated embedding spaces.
The result is a tropical, quiver-theoretic, ergodic framework for architecture design and search that is intended to be both mathematically serious and practically actionable.
